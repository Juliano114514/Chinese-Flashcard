# Five-stage semantic grading, 2026-10-05

All 9,223 entries must be read and assigned individually. Classify the lexical item as written, together with its recorded meaning, expected familiarity, register and specialist background. Do not carry forward old rarity or use a frequency rank as the decision. No equal-size quotas. These are practical learning bands inferred from Chinese native-language educational expectations, not an official nationwide per-grade word list.

0: Grade 1 primary school. Basic concrete objects, close family, food/body/animals, simple daily actions/feelings/time/counting, elementary classroom words and basic high-frequency function words. A six-to-seven-year-old can understand the recorded meaning without a specialist concept. Do not put sophisticated idioms, adult finance or abstract politics here just because common characters are used.
1: Grade 7 / first year of middle school. General everyday and school vocabulary beyond the very basic band; simple social concepts, common expressions and transparent common idioms; introductory ordinary science/history concepts. This band also covers primary grades 2-6 content because no intermediate band exists.
2: Grade 10 / first year of high school. General formal prose, abstract social/moral/emotional concepts, common literary idioms or allusions, standard high-school science/economics/civics terminology. This band also covers middle school grades 8-9 content.
3: First-year general university. Advanced formal/academic/social/administrative terms and specialized non-language subjects that require adult or tertiary conceptual background; technical science, law, medicine, finance, computing, engineering. Rare technical words outside Chinese language/literature are 3, not automatically 4.
4: First-year Chinese language/literature major. Specialist language/linguistics/literary analysis, classical written vocabulary, uncommon classical allusions, traditional philological/cultural/literary words whose sense needs close literary or linguistic study. Rare everyday words alone do not imply 4. Common literary devices met in school remain 1/2; difficult literary terms or classical expressions can be 4.

Calibration: assess vocabulary familiarity and recognition, not merely how simple the denoted object is. An archaic name for an everyday object (for example 氍毹 or 萝菔) still needs literary/classical vocabulary exposure; a rare botanical/zoological/medical taxon can require tertiary specialist vocabulary even if its example is a simple observation. Familiar everyday species and foods remain basic/general. A generic example sentence must not erase the lexical item's register or terminology. Formal bodily or connective vocabulary need not share the same level as its basic colloquial equivalent. Single-character spelling or unfamiliarity alone does not determine a grade; combine word exposure, recorded sense and domain.
